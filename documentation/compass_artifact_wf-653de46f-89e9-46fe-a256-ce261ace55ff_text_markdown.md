# A hackathon map for Dutch subsidy access

**An AI-powered subsidy navigator is arguably the strongest "Build Local" idea on the table in Amsterdam right now.** The Centraal Planbureau confirms Dutch citizens leave **more than €1 billion/year unclaimed** in just three national toeslagen, and broader aggregates put total non-take-up at €1.5–2 billion. The landscape is split across **8 main agencies, 342 municipalities and hundreds of private funds** running an estimated 2,000–3,000 regelingen. Existing tools (Berekenuwrecht, Geldfit, Pak je kans) are fragmented, Dutch-only, form-based and miss huge categories. No consumer-facing Dutch LLM product unifies them. Meanwhile, clean APIs exist for roughly 70% of the data a hackathon team needs (Amsterdam DSO-API, CVDR/KOOP, CBS OData, KVK test-key), and Yivi is a realistic identity alternative to DigiD. The winning hackathon play is a multilingual, RAG-grounded chat agent that turns a life-event ("I just had a baby", "Ik ben mijn baan kwijt") into a ranked list of concrete, citable benefits with auto-filled application drafts — with an explicit human-in-the-loop handoff.

## The landscape is deeply fragmented by design

Dutch income support is structurally split. **The Belastingdienst pays four toeslagen** — zorgtoeslag (≤€131/mnd single, ~4.6M households), huurtoeslag (max rent €900/mnd in 2025, ~1.5M households), kindgebonden budget (up to €2,511/kind/yr under 12; alleenstaande-ouderkop adds ~€3,400), and kinderopvangtoeslag (~600K households, moving to 96% income-independent in 2026). **SVB runs AOW and AIO-aanvulling** (~€1,413/mnd single, ~48K recipients). **UWV runs WW/WIA/Wajong/TW, DUO runs studiefinanciering**, zorgverzekeraars deliver Zvw, CAK collects Wlz bijdragen, and **342 gemeenten operate Participatiewet, bijzondere bijstand, individuele inkomenstoeslag, Wmo, Jeugdwet, minima-AV and kwijtschelding**. Amsterdam alone runs roughly 30 minimavoorzieningen inside its Brede Monitor Armoederegelingen.

Amsterdam's headline schemes anchor the local layer. **Stadspas (groene stip)** reaches about **280,000 Amsterdammers** and in schooljaar 2025–26 delivers a kindtegoed of €125 (0–3 yrs) rising to **€475 (12–14 yrs)**. The Witgoedregeling funds emergency white-goods replacement in-kind (~€400–€800), the collective Zilveren Kruis **AV Amsterdam** (basispremie €156/mnd in 2025) is the city's minima-zorgverzekering, and **kwijtschelding of afvalstoffen- and rioolheffing** reaches ~50,000 households yearly. Energietoeslag, a €1,300 lifeline in 2023, does **not exist in 2024 or 2025** — a critical, often-missed fact.

For kleine stichtingen, the funding stack is a matrix of ~25 public cultural funds + hundreds of private vermogensfondsen. **AFK project grants range from €1,500 (cat. 1) to >€17,500 (cat. 4)**, Oranje Fonds typically €5–25K, VSBfonds €5–50K (4-month lead time), Kansfonds €2,500–25K, Fonds 21 €5–75K, Prins Bernhard Cultuurfonds up to €50K, and **stadsdeel Buurtbudget initiatives usually €2,500–€5,000** (stadsdeel Zuid max €3,000). The SCP/Divosa estimate of **2,000–3,000 regelingen nationwide** — against no central machine-readable register — is itself the core problem the hackathon is attacking.

## Non-take-up is a billion-euro market failure

The best evidence comes from the **CPB study *Onbenut recht* (2023, data from 2021)**, which quantifies non-use household-by-household: **12% of eligible households miss zorgtoeslag (679,000 households, €632M unclaimed, avg €72/mnd)**, 12% miss huurtoeslag (196K households, €246M, median €77/mnd), and 8.5% miss kindgebonden budget (99K households, €139M). Private-sector renters fare twice as badly as corporatiehuurders (20% vs 10% non-use). Langdurig non-use is staggering: **56% of zorgtoeslag-eligible non-users stay out for 4 consecutive years**, missing ~€3,800; huurtoeslag non-claimers miss ~€7,200 over the same period.

AIO is the textbook worst-case. The Algemene Rekenkamer's *Ouderdomsregelingen ontleed* (2019) found **48–56% of AIO-eligible elderly never claim** — 34–51K households. An OIS Amsterdam study found only **22% of elderly with incomplete AOW had ever heard of AIO**, and 71% of those respondents had a non-Western migration background. The Nationale Ombudsman reported in 2024 that **about two-thirds of Wajongers skip the Toeslagenwet supplement** they're entitled to. There is no reliable national figure for bijzondere bijstand non-use, but Movisie and G40 gemeenten repeatedly flag that strict vermogensgrenzen (~€1,000 single) exclude many rechthebbenden before they even apply.

**Barriers are as much psychological and cognitive as procedural.** The WRR's landmark *Weten is nog geen doen* (2017) introduced the "doenvermogen" concept and now anchors a mandatory doenvermogentoets in the Beleidskompas. Layered on top: **2.5 million Dutch adults are laaggeletterd** (3M on the PIAAC 2024 definition), costing society €1.13B/yr (PwC); **3.4 million adults cannot independently handle digital government** per Movisie/University of Twente; and **300,000 people are both laaggeletterd and digitally weak**. The toeslagenaffaire left durable scarring — **71% of Dutch citizens reported reduced trust in government** (I&O Research, 2021) and Ombudsman Van Zutphen notes people at the social minimum are now "kopschuw" because **2.3 million of the ~8 million annual toeslag-toekenningen lead to terugvordering** (CBS via Simonse, 2024).

Amsterdam magnifies the migration dimension: **59% of Amsterdammers have a migratieachtergrond** (2023), rising to 67% among 30–40-year-olds; **174 nationalities** live in the city and 174,000 residents arrived within the last decade. Pharos estimates **2.5M NL residents do not speak Dutch as a first language**, tolkvergoeding was abolished in 2012, and Pharos' 2024 field research on Bulgarian migrants in Zaandam-Oost describes a dual failure mode — malafide intermediaries exploit people *and* rechthebbenden miss voorzieningen entirely.

Kleine stichtingen face a parallel complexity tax: projectplan + begroting + dekkingsplan + jaarrekening + statuten + KvK-uittreksel per application, **3–5 months lead time**, cofinancieringseisen around 25%, and **accountantsverklaring verplicht boven €50,000** — a threshold that kills most small applicants before they start. VWS even runs a dedicated "Ontregelprojecten" subsidy to reduce the reporting burden it created elsewhere.

## Existing tools cover only slivers of the problem

The Dutch consumer-tool stack is useful but structurally incomplete. **Berekenuwrecht/Bereken Je Recht (Nibud + Stimulansz)** is the most comprehensive calculator but only activates the full municipal layer in licensed gemeenten and is being re-platformed in 2025. **Geldfit.nl's "Potjes-check"** completes in 15 minutes and finds an average **€462/household** — valuable, but national-toeslagen-heavy with thin fonds coverage. **De VoorzieningenWijzer** (used by gemeenten/woningcorporaties) is effective but consultant-led, not self-service. **Regelhulp.nl** is a VWS wegwijzer without calculations. **MijnOverheid** is a passive inbox. **Amsterdam's "Pak je kans"** unified form is the best local example — 11 local regelingen in 6 languages — but still reactive and city-scoped. Human help through Sociaal Raadslieden, Humanitas Thuisadministratie and SchuldHulpMaatje is excellent in quality and catastrophically undersupplied in quantity.

On the fonds side, **Vindsubsidies.nl, Subsidiefocus and Fondsenboek (FIN)** are paywalled B2B databases without eligibility screening. Kansenkaart (Erasmus/EUR) is a research dashboard, not a finder. There is **no consumer equivalent of the UK's Turn2us grants search**.

The Dutch government AI wave is real but almost entirely internal. TNO's Overheidsbrede Monitor GenAI (2025) counted **~72 genAI applications across government — up from 8 in 2024**, with 34 at gemeente level. The highest-profile products — **Gem** (~25 gemeenten), **Guus** (Goes), **Robin** (Wijchen), **ChatRWS, vlam-chat/vlam-search** (SSC-ICT, 26,500 ambtenaren in 2026), **GPT-NL** (€13.5M sovereign LLM) — are FAQ-style or civil-servant-facing. iBestuur's 2025 critique is telling: *"no machine discovers coherence across 20 toeslagen/subsidies/regelingen from many organizations — because the coherence doesn't exist."* **No prominent Dutch consumer-facing LLM benefits-finder has launched.** Internationally, **mRelief, Propel (5M+ users), Code for America's GetCalFresh**, and the **Benefits Data Trust + Nava "Benefit Navigator"** (LLM chatbot for caseworkers covering ~40 programs, multilingual, with RAG citations) are the references to borrow from. UK equivalents **Entitledto, Turn2us and Policy in Practice** are calculator-driven, not conversational.

## The data stack is 70% buildable in 24 hours

The highest-feasibility APIs form a workable MVP. **Amsterdam DSO-API (`api.data.amsterdam.nl/v1/`)** exposes the Openbaar Subsidieregister, BAG, BBGA neighborhood stats and maatschappelijke organisaties in clean REST/HAL-JSON (free API key, 5-min form). **CBS Open Data / StatLine** via OData + the `cbsodata` Python library gives neighborhood income and demographics — your proxy layer for eligibility where Belastingdienst data isn't available. **KOOP/overheid.nl SRU endpoints** expose the Basis Wetten Bestand (45K regelingen in XML) and — crucially — the **CVDR/DROP register of decentrale regelgeving** where gemeentelijke subsidieverordeningen actually live. **KVK's `/zoeken?type=rechtspersoon`** returns stichtingen directly; the free test-key lets you demo without a bank account. **developer.overheid.nl** indexes ~140 government REST APIs with a single free key.

The blockers are equally clear. **DigiD is not feasible at a hackathon** (Logius contract, BSN-autorisatielijst, OIN, PKIoverheid-cert, weeks of process). **eHerkenning is similarly out.** **SVB, UWV and Belastingdienst have no open citizen APIs.** RVO's subsidie-feed is XML-only and effectively requires scraping. Private fondsen (VSBfonds, Oranje Fonds) have no APIs whatsoever.

The practical workaround is **Yivi (formerly IRMA)** — open-source, self-hostable, free, demo-ready in Docker. It can disclose BSN, naam, DOB, address and KVK-number as verified attributes in a privacy-preserving way. Combined with the KVK test-key, that's a plausible demo identity flow. For the language model layer, **Fietje-2-chat (2.7B, Apache-2.0) for fast demo and GEITje-7B-ultra for quality** are the Dutch-native options on HuggingFace, though Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B now beat them on ScandEval Dutch NLG — use `intfloat/multilingual-e5-large` for embeddings.

A defensible 24-hour stack: Next.js/FastAPI, Qdrant or pgvector, LlamaIndex/Haystack for RAG, scraped-and-cached ingestion of **RVO + CVDR + AFK/Oranjefonds/Kansfonds pages + Amsterdam minima-regelingen pages + Belastingdienst toeslagenkaart 2025**, with a Yivi + KVK-test identity layer.

## Where the biggest opportunity sits

The sharpest gap is the complete absence of a **proactive, conversational, multilingual, source-cited agent** that spans **all fragmented sources** and **hands off to a human when stakes are high**. Every design lever maps to a documented pain point:

- **Life-event triggers over forms.** CPB shows 56% of non-users miss zorgtoeslag 4 years running. "Ik ben werkloos / mijn kind is geboren / ik ben verhuisd naar Amsterdam" is a better UI than rent-in-euros-per-month.
- **Multilingual voice/chat.** 59% of Amsterdam has a migratieachtergrond; 2.5M NL residents don't have Dutch as L1; only Amsterdam's "Pak je kans" offers 6 languages today. An LLM removes this wall overnight.
- **Document extraction and auto-fill.** Upload a loonstrook, huurcontract and beschikking; prefill Stadspas, huurtoeslag and kwijtschelding in parallel. The current status quo is manual re-entry across three portals.
- **Source-grounded, not hallucinated.** Georgetown Beeck Center's 2024–25 experiments show LLMs confidently hallucinate eligibility. Every answer must cite its regeling-text (RAG over CVDR + rijksoverheid.nl). Under the EU AI Act this is a high-risk use case; citations are the defence.
- **Conservative mode to defuse terugvordering-anxiety.** Model an 80% voorschot scenario and explain consequences in plain B1-niveau Dutch, directly addressing the psychological scar of the toeslagenaffaire.

**The highest-ROI "quick wins"** are **AIO-aanvulling** (~50% non-use, straightforward criteria: AOW + sociaal minimum), **zorgtoeslag for self-employed** (20% non-use, deterministic income test), **huurtoeslag in the private-rent segment** (20% non-use, clear rent+income rules), and **Amsterdam Stadspas + kindtegoed** (one of the best-documented entitlements with known eligibility thresholds and an existing unified form to plug into). Covering just those four would touch **>800,000 Dutch households and hundreds of millions of euros** annually — and every single one is rule-based enough to be handled reliably by a well-prompted LLM with RAG over the actual regeling-text.

## The 24-hour winning build

A realistic demo architecture: a chat front-end in Dutch/English/Arabic/Turkish/Ukrainian that asks **one life-event question**, ingests **one optional document** (loonstrook or beschikking), verifies identity via **Yivi demo-scheme** (BSN + address) and KVK-test for stichting users, queries a vector index of **CVDR + Amsterdam subsidieregister + RVO + belastingdienst.nl + ~10 fonds sites**, and returns a **ranked card list** of entitlements with euro estimates, citations, a one-click "prefill application" for Stadspas/huurtoeslag/kwijtschelding/AIO, and a visible "Talk to a Sociaal Raadslid" escalation. For stichtingen the same engine matches a project description to AFK/Oranjefonds/VSBfonds/Kansfonds with grant-size filtering via KVK rechtspersoon-lookup.

## Conclusion

The Dutch subsidy system is a clear-cut policy failure masquerading as procedural complexity, and that is exactly why an AI-native interface can leapfrog it. The numbers — **>€1B unclaimed CPB-confirmed, >€1.5–2B estimated, 2.5M laaggeletterd, 3.4M digital-weak, 2.3M terugvorderingen per year, 59% Amsterdam migration background** — are not a problem statement, they are an addressable market. The building blocks to hit it in a weekend exist: Amsterdam DSO-API, CVDR via KOOP-SRU, CBS OData, KVK test-key, Yivi, Fietje-2 and GEITje. The dominant incumbents are form-based calculators written for Dutch-speaking digitally literate users; nothing in the landscape is both conversational and comprehensive. A 24-hour team that builds a multilingual, life-event-triggered, RAG-grounded agent with visible citations and human-handoff — and demonstrates it end-to-end on **AIO, Stadspas, huurtoeslag and zorgtoeslag** — will not just win a hackathon. It will show a path to closing the most expensive non-take-up problem in the Dutch social contract.
